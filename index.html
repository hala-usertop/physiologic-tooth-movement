02 · Mechanisms

What actually moves the tooth?

Ten Cate presents eruption as a coordinated tissue process. No single mechanism should be treated as the sole “engine” of eruption.

The tissue-remodeling model

INTERACTIVE

Simplified teaching diagram — not to scale. It emphasizes the coordinated relationship between the dental follicle, alveolar bone remodeling, and tooth movement.

The follicle participates in signaling that coordinates bone remodeling around the erupting tooth, including recruitment and differentiation of osteoclast-lineage cells. It should be viewed as a major regulator, not an isolated mechanical “motor.”
Osteoclast recruitment permits bone removal along the eruption pathway, while bone formation occurs in other regions. Eruption therefore involves coupled tissue destruction and formation.
PDL fibroblasts, collagen turnover, vascularity and tissue remodeling contribute to the changing relationship between tooth and socket. PDL activity is one component of a multifactorial process.
Root formation accompanies the eruptive process, but experimental and developmental evidence supports a broader multifactorial model rather than a simple “root pushes tooth out” mechanism.
03 · Histology lens

Follow the cells

🧬

Reduced enamel epithelium

Participates in formation of the eruption pathway and epithelial events around the erupting crown.

🦴

Osteoclast lineage

Removes alveolar bone where remodeling is required, allowing a path of reduced resistance.

🏗️

Osteoblasts

Build bone in regions undergoing compensatory remodeling and socket adaptation.

🧵

PDL fibroblasts

Maintain and remodel collagen-rich PDL while the tooth changes position.

Molecular coordination — keep it simple

Follicular signals

The dental follicle participates in signaling that attracts and supports osteoclast differentiation.

CSF-1 / IL-1 pathway

Ten Cate describes signaling involving CSF-1 and IL-1 in the regulation of osteoclast-related events during eruption.

Path of least resistance

Connective-tissue and bone remodeling help establish a route through which the tooth can erupt.

04 · Clinical bridge

Why does this matter?

ORTHODONTICS

PDL + bone plasticity

The same remodeling capacity of the PDL and alveolar bone helps explain how controlled orthodontic forces can move teeth.

DEVELOPMENT

Eruption disturbances

Disturbances in the eruption pathway, surrounding tissues, or developmental timing can alter normal eruption.

PRIMARY DENTITION

Shedding

Physiologic exfoliation involves progressive root resorption and weakening of periodontal attachment before loss of the primary tooth.

High-yield take-home points

  • 1. Physiologic tooth movement is a continuous, coordinated tissue process.
  • 2. Eruption is multifactorial — avoid reducing it to a single “pushing force.”
  • 3. The dental follicle is a major regulatory/signaling tissue for eruption-associated remodeling.
  • 4. Bone resorption and formation occur in coordinated regions around the developing tooth.
  • 5. Post-eruptive movement helps accommodate growth and functional wear.
  • 6. Shedding of primary teeth is associated with progressive root resorption and loss of attachment.
